# How to Read the AI Manifold Map

AI Manifold is not a ranking, a recommendation list, or a prediction of the future.
It is a navigational artifact designed to help users orient themselves in a rapidly
evolving AI ecosystem without reducing it to premature judgments.

This document explains how to read the map correctly — and how *not* to read it.

---

## 1. What the map represents

The map represents **relationships**, not verdicts.

Each AI tool appears in relation to:
- professional or productive contexts (clusters),
- other tools,
- and its perceived relevance over time.

The goal is **orientation**, not evaluation.

---

## 2. Radial structure and distance

- **Distance is relational**, not hierarchical.
- Tools that appear closer to a cluster are *more commonly used or referenced* in that context.
- Tools that appear between clusters reflect **cross-contextual use**.

Important:
> Distance does **not** mean quality, superiority, or intelligence.

There is no “top”, “best”, or “winner” position on this map.

---

## 3. Clusters and multiple belonging

Clusters represent **productive or professional contexts**, not market segments.

A tool may belong to multiple clusters because:
- it is used differently in different contexts,
- it supports distinct workflows,
- or it acts as a bridge between domains.

This is intentional.

> Forced single classification reduces understanding.
> Multiple belonging improves navigation.

---

## 4. Size, weight, and visibility

If size or visual weight is present:
- it reflects **relative prominence or frequency of use within a context**,
- often derived from longitudinal observation or user input.

It does **not** represent:
- model capability,
- technical superiority,
- commercial success,
- or future dominance.

Visibility is contextual, not absolute.

---

## 5. Longitudinal meaning

AI Manifold is designed to be **longitudinal**.

This means:
- positions may change over time,
- clusters may evolve,
- tools may gain or lose relevance in specific contexts.

Each version of the map is a **snapshot**, not a final state.

> The map should be read as a moving landscape, not a fixed taxonomy.

---

## 6. What the map is NOT

To avoid misinterpretation, the map is explicitly **not**:

- a ranking of AI tools
- a buyer’s guide
- an endorsement or recommendation
- a prediction of technological dominance
- a measure of “intelligence” or “quality”

Any reading that turns the map into one of the above is a misuse.

---

## 7. How to use the map productively

The map is most useful when used to:

- explore unfamiliar parts of the AI ecosystem
- understand how tools relate across domains
- identify bridges between professional contexts
- support learning and orientation
- ask better questions before making decisions

It is not designed to give answers, but to **improve the questions you ask**.

---

## 8. A simple reading example

If a tool appears:
- near multiple clusters → it likely plays a transversal role
- far from the center → it may be specialized or emerging
- closer to one cluster than another → its primary use is contextual, not absolute

The correct question is never:
> “Is this tool better?”

But rather:
> “Better for *what*, and *in which context*?”

---

## 9. Final note on interpretation

AI Manifold assumes that:
- orientation precedes optimization,
- context precedes judgment,
- and navigation precedes evaluation.

If the map helps you avoid getting lost — even briefly — it has fulfilled its purpose.
