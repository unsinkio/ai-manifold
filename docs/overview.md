
# 🧭 How to Read the Radial AI Map

This radial map is a **cognitive navigation tool**, not a market landscape or a ranking.
Its purpose is to help readers understand **how AI tools relate to domains, time, and use trajectories**, rather than listing features or popularity.

Think of it as a **radar for sense-making**.

---

## 1. Overall Structure

The map is organized along **three orthogonal dimensions**:

1. **Radial distance (center → outward)**
2. **Angular position (sectors)**
3. **Concentric rings (time)**

Each dimension encodes a different kind of information.

---

## 2. Radial Distance — Generality vs. Specialization

* **Center**
  Represents **foundational or transversal AI tools**.
  These tools are not tied to a single industry and can project into multiple domains.

* **Outer rings**
  Represent **domain-specialized applications**.
  The farther from the center, the more contextual and constrained the tool becomes.

> Rule of thumb:
> **Closer to center = higher flexibility, higher cognitive responsibility.**
> **Farther out = higher specialization, lower transferability.**

---

## 3. Angular Sectors — Domains of Human Activity

Each sector corresponds to a **field of practice**, not a rigid industry:

* Design, Media & Content
* Education & LMS / SIS
* Productivity & Knowledge Work
* Sales, Marketing & Real Estate
* Finance, Accounting & Audit
* Health, Clinics & Wellness
* Research & Science
* Engineering, DevOps & Development

These sectors answer the question:
**“In what kind of human work does this AI naturally operate?”**

---

## 4. Concentric Rings — Time & Maturity

The dotted rings (2018 → 2024) represent **when AI meaningfully entered or reshaped that domain**.

Important:

* This is **not progress or quality**
* It indicates **emergence and consolidation over time**

Older does not mean better.
Newer does not mean superior.
It only answers *when* a domain became AI-active.

---

## 5. Points — Individual AI Tools

Each labeled point represents a **specific AI product or system**.

* Points near the center indicate **cross-domain tools**
* Points inside sector arcs indicate **domain-native tools**

Examples visible on the map:

* Notion AI
* Cengage MindTap AI
* GoHighLevel AI
* Fathom AI

Their position reflects **how they are used**, not how they are marketed.

---

## 6. Lines — Usage Trajectories (Not Dependencies)

Lines connecting points to sectors are **intentional and directional**.

They do **not** mean:

* ownership
* technical integration
* dependency

They mean:

> **“This tool commonly projects into this domain when solving real problems.”**

Example:

* A tool may originate as a productivity system but project strongly into Sales or Education.
* Another may be domain-native and remain contained.

Lines represent **movement of use**, not category membership.

---

## 7. What This Map Is *Not*

* ❌ Not a ranking
* ❌ Not a vendor comparison
* ❌ Not a roadmap
* ❌ Not an endorsement

If you are looking for “best tools”, this map is the wrong instrument.

---

## 8. What This Map *Is*

* ✔ A **navigation aid** for complex AI ecosystems
* ✔ A way to reason about **tool generality vs. specialization**
* ✔ A visualization of **how humans move with AI across contexts**
* ✔ A guard against collapsing *thinking* into *tool choice*

---

## 9. How to Use This Map

Use this map to ask better questions:

* *Is this problem domain-specific or transversal?*
* *Should I start from a central tool or a specialized one?*
* *Am I navigating domains, or just stacking tools?*
* *What changes when a tool crosses sectors?*

The map does not give answers.
It **prevents category errors**.

---

## 10. Core Principle

> **The map does not evaluate what you know.
> It evaluates how you orient yourself when the map is incomplete.**

That is its purpose.
